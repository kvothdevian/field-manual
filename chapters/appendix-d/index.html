§ gaps & next steps

Gaps & next steps — honest, prioritized

No true gaps (zero ●). Five dimensions are ◐ thin by design — each with why we cut, what exists, and what a deeper pass would add. This is the living “next” list.

thin · keep as pointer — not a gap

LSP diagnostics in agent loop

Ch02 + Ch04 note lsp: true off-by-default and the lint-CLI tradeoff. No full lab. Why thin: most harnesses run lint CLIs faster than LSP for agentic edits; LSP cost deserves its own benchmark. Next: a measured bake-off (LSP vs eslint --fix vs tsc --noEmit) on Build 3 with token + time deltas — App A addendum, not a new chapter.

Ch02Ch04App Aeffort: half-day
thin · next step sized

Licensing & copyright of output

Ch14 and Ch15 flag “who owns AI output” with a ToS pointer, not legal depth. Why thin: jurisdiction-specific and fast-moving — a definitive answer today misleads tomorrow. Next: one-page “ask your org” checklist + 3 receipted pointers (USCO guidance, GitHub Copilot ToS, provider terms delta table) as a boxed addition to Ch14. No legal advice, just triage.

Ch14Ch15effort: half-day + counsel review
thin · exercise exists, lab deferred

Advanced multi-agent orchestration at scale

Ch06 teaches fan-out + subagents; Ch05 shows worktrees; Ch16 ties a daily habit. No long-running orchestrator (queue, supervisor, handoff). Why thin: the book's ladder is single-human scale — 3 parallel agents, not fleets. Next: a Build 3 “night-shift” recipe: 3 subagents + file locks + one merge gate, measured for thrash — fits as a Beyond block in Ch06, not a new chapter.

Ch06Ch16effort: weekend
thin · deliberate compactness

Deep benchmark forensics

Ch07's calibration game teaches you to spot gaming in minutes; App B's ledger grades every claim T1/T2/T3. No full “reproduce METR/SWE-bench” tutorial. Why thin: reproducing a bench well is a week of compute + hygiene — the craft is triage, not replication. Next: a 10-minute “claim autopsy” worksheet + link to METR's public data pack as a printable one-pager (App C cross-link).

next · not yet in the book

Voice, product discovery, and non-code agentic work

Outside the book's promise — we teach agentic coding. Interviews, synthesis, and product discovery with agents are not covered. Next: if requested, a short interlude (not a chapter) mapping the same loop (aim → watch → verify) onto discovery — with receipts from practitioner interviews, not benchmarks.

out of scope v1 · tracked as request
how we decide

Gate for promoting a thin cut → primary

A thin cut becomes a primary only when (1) ≥3 readers hit the same wall, (2) a T1 receipt exists for the fix, and (3) a build can demo it end-to-end. Otherwise it stays a pointer — the book refuses to swell to cover everything shallowly.

why this section exists

An exhaustive book must show its edges. “No gaps” would be a lie — there are always thinner cuts. Naming them is the exhaustiveness proof; hiding them is how scope rots.
