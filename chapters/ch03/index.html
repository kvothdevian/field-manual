Briefing · 5 min · the four doors and the one rule

i.Four doors, one rule

Every $0 path into a frontier model is one of four doors. Door 1, subscription reuse: point opencode at a subscription you already pay for — ChatGPT Plus/Pro OAuth, GitHub Copilot device flow, GitLab Duo — and the model bill stays zero. Door 2, free tiers: gratis cloud allowances fenced by rate limits. Door 3, cheap open-weight APIs: near-free inference where a million tokens costs cents and off-peak halves it again. Door 4, fully local: Ollama or LM Studio on your machine — private and offline-capable, paid in hardware.

The one rule governing all four: free tiers are funnels, not gifts. Every gratis allowance is rate-limited — requests and tokens per minute and day, with 429s as the walls. Your job tonight is not to memorize numbers (they rot monthly) but to learn the method: open each door, read its actual limits page, and measure the wall yourself. The numbers here were re-verified live on 2026-09-03; treat them as fresh, not permanent.

Two warnings before you touch anything. First, the corpse: GitHub Models is dead — retired July 30, 2026, playground and catalog gone. Any guide still listing it is stale, and this is what stale looks like. Second, the training tradeoff: several $0 doors fund themselves with your prompts — OpenRouter gates training-capable providers behind your privacy toggle, Gemini's unpaid tier is an unverified lead (confirm in ai.google.dev terms), and the Mistral clause below was cut when its article 404'd on re-verification day. Free means read the terms, not no cost.

Understand the funnel economics and the walls stop surprising you. Free tiers convert: OpenRouter's daily cap jumps 50→1,000 the moment you've bought $10 of credits lifetime — the funnel's whole job is that first $10. Kimi auto-unlocks higher limits with cumulative spend; Google's tiers graduate you automatically as billing history matures. The vendors are not hiding this; the limits pages are the business model, published. Free is a strategy, not a loyalty.

The training tradeoff decides which door your code may enter. OpenRouter surfaces per-provider train-on-prompts flags with separate paid/free opt-outs — privacy's real price is losing the cheapest providers. Gemini's unpaid tier is an unverified lead (confirm in ai.google.dev terms first). The Mistral clause was cut when its article 404'd — the freshness rule eating its own cooking. Standing order: find the training clause before the first real prompt, set the toggle, never route proprietary code through an unread door.

1.Door 1 — subscription reuse (reader-run)

Goal: zero new bills by spending subscriptions already held. opencode documents OAuth setup for ChatGPT Plus/Pro, GitHub Copilot device flow, and GitLab Duo — "zero setup" in the maintainers' words.

Needs a subscription you hold — nothing to execute here without yours, so this door is reader-run by design. What "zero setup" actually means: opencode auth login opens the provider's OAuth flow (ChatGPT in the browser, Copilot device code you paste at github.com/login/device), tokens land in opencode's auth file, and every later session spends the plan's quota instead of API dollars. Note the removed exhibit while you're on the providers page: Claude Pro/Max proxy plugins were pulled in v1.3.0 because Anthropic explicitly prohibits them. 3.Door 3 — open-weight APIs, the cents door

Goal: learn off-peak and cache economics on DeepSeek's live pricing (re-verified today), then wire any cheap API as reader-run.

The worked example, from today's fetch: DeepSeek V4-Flash off-peak input $0.22/M tokens, output $0.66/M, cache-hit input $0.007/M — off-peak is half of peak, and peak is weekday mornings UTC. A million-token agent session at off-peak with warm cache costs single cents. Kimi's ladder rhymes: K2.7 Code $0.95/$4.00 with $0.19 cache hits, higher limits unlocking with cumulative spend. The method transfers: read the pricing grid, find the cache column, schedule bulk work off-peak.

Do the cents math once, by hand, so it sticks. Take a 700K-token agent session on V4-Flash off-peak: 500K input tokens at 80% cache hit, 200K output. Cache misses cost 100K × $0.22/M = $0.022; cache hits cost 400K × $0.007/M = $0.0028; output costs 200K × $0.66/M = $0.132. Total: about $0.16. The same session at peak without cache discipline costs roughly five times more — and the entire difference is two habits: warm cache (repeat context, don't re-paste it) and off-peak scheduling. That $0.16 number is also your negotiation baseline: any subscription must beat your measured cents, not your imagined scale.

4.Door 4 — local, docs-only and labeled

The economics are hardware, not tokens: private, offline-capable, rate-limit-free — paid in VRAM and patience.
