Chapter 15: Costs, limits & grey zones

facts checked as of 2026-08-26 · every rate number below expires — re-stamp before betting a habit on it

Promise: one honest reference. Read any provider's rate-limit page like an engineer, cap your worst-month spend in advance, name what free tiers charge in data, and price a grey-zone shortcut before taking it — compact enough to consult, framed so you rarely need most of it.

This is the book's only money-forward chapter by design — every other chapter stays about craft. Everything providers throw at you reduces to three dials and one hidden price:

  • Burst. Requests per minute — a token bucket. Drain it faster than it refills and you get HTTP 429 with a retry-after header whose size names the wall: seconds = minute-wall, hours = rolling window, midnight = daily.
  • Throughput. Tokens per minute, often split input/output. Agent contexts are huge, so token walls bite long before request counts do.
  • Budget. Spend caps stop you at a dollar figure; prepaid credits stop at zero; shared subscription pools refill monthly.
  • The hidden price: data. Free tiers are frequently paid in training rights over your prompts. A written opt-out beats silence; silence beats a forum post.

Grey zones are the same arithmetic on shortcuts: risk = probability × consequence — suspension, credential loss, silently worse output. Not morality — pricing; score = calibration, not morality.

Tactic 1 · Tier stacking across providers

Hold free or paid tiers at several providers at once; route each job to whichever door fits.

stability
legality

~ shaky ✓ sanctioned

Allowed — until a term says otherwise. Nothing forbids being many providers' customer at once. But hosters reserve suspension rights for disproportionate burden, Pages bars commercial-business hosting, and Actions bars standalone services — contractual text, effective 2026-04-27. Legal today, revocable overnight.

Tactic 2 · Open-weight self-hosting

Download open model weights and run them on your own hardware, outside any hosted API.

stability
legality

✓ rock-solid ✓ sanctioned

The clean path. Nobody's terms attach because nobody else is involved — nothing leaves your machine, so training clauses and rate walls never apply. Guides frame self-hosting MIT-licensed weights as exactly this escape hatch from residency and policy risk (a lead, not settled doctrine). The honest cost: your RAM/VRAM and context length become the ceiling.

Tactic 3 · Cheap off-peak routing

Schedule heavy jobs into hours when tokens bill cheaper — night-rate windows like DeepSeek's off-peak discounts.

stability
legality

✓ rock-solid ✓ sanctioned

Pure arithmetic. Vendors publish discount windows because they want demand shifted into them — you comply with the pricing page, not evade it. z.ai's official usage policy bakes dynamic peak/off-peak limits into its plan; community writeups quote off-peak billing at half points. It only feels exploitative because the savings look big — that is the vendor telling you when capacity idles.

Point your harness at a subscription you already pay for, authenticating with your account sign-in instead of an API key.

Violates — and dies overnight. The walls, with receipts

Anthropic's published grid is the canonical token-bucket read: paid access buys taller walls, not the absence of walls — per-model-class RPM plus input/output tokens-per-minute ceilings (Start-tier Opus 5: 1,000 RPM / 2M ITPM / 400K OTPM) and monthly spend caps ($500 Start / $1k Build / $200k Scale). One clause rewards discipline: cached-read input doesn't count toward ITPM — prompt caching widens your throughput walls for free. The ritual transfers to any provider: find the four numbers (burst RPM, input TPM, output TPM, spend cap), write them down, stamp the date.

§2

The meters: shared pools and a number to distrust politely

Codex shows where subscription meters are heading: included across ChatGPT plans from Free to Pro, all drawing one shared allowance pool, billed since 2026-04-02 in token credits, not per-message. OpenAI cites roughly $100–200 per developer-month as average. Apply Chapter 07 first: vendor-reported, no distribution or workload definition, from a party selling the product. Treat it as order-of-magnitude — professional agentic usage costs more than pizza money — then verify against your own /status.

§3

The hidden price: what free tiers charge in data

OpenRouter prints the trade in writing: :free models run 50 requests/day (1,000/day once you hold ≥$10 lifetime credits); prompts are not logged by default; providers that may train on your prompts route only if your privacy settings permit — with separate opt-outs for paid and free models. The nuance: opting out can exclude the cheapest providers — some doors are cheap because your prompts are part of the payment.

Contrast z.ai's GLM Coding Plan: its official usage policy documents concurrency tiers and dynamic peak/off-peak limits — and says nothing about input-training. That silence is citable: there is no written opt-out to point at. Community reporting (T3 — a labeled lead) adds terms permitting training on your data plus April 2026 enforcement limiting use to "coding scenarios" via automated detection: throttle → suspend → permanent ban at three violations, errors 1302/1303 reported. A matching fence surrounds Gemini and Mistral: third-party writeups report unpaid Gemini tiers may train on prompts and responses — verify against ai.google.dev's terms before relying (that verification workflow is the lesson) — and Mistral's free tier may train unless opted out in the Admin Console, paid tiers defaulted opted-out. Until you've read a policy yourself: private code goes through written-opt-out doors or local weights.

§4

What you own when the output ships

One line from the US Copyright Office (AI report, Part 2, 2025-01-29): human authorship is required. Purely machine-generated material is not copyrightable; work you selected, arranged, or modified can be. Keep evidence of your editorial hand — the more autonomous the run, the thinner your claim. Training-side questions were still pre-publication at our check.

tip

Prefer doors whose terms you've actually read. Ten minutes with a vendor's rate-limit and privacy pages beats a hundred forum threads — the person who read the terms is never surprised by them.

warning

Every unofficial shortcut is a countdown. Not "might break someday" — scheduled to. Maintainers warn in their own READMEs, vendors enforce, uptime proves nothing. Never let anything undocumented become load-bearing.

Where this lands in your build

The agent-cost-compass exists because these numbers move. Vendors reprice without notice; tiers appear and vanish between printings of any static page — including this one. So the compass is this chapter's living artifact, not its copy: every constant here enters Build 1 as a row with an as-of stamp and a recheck date, and the compass's whole job is surfacing drift in minutes instead of mid-session.

§6

Try it yourself

  1. Exact-click checkpoint. Open https://docs.anthropic.com/en/api/rate-limits, find the tier nearest your reality, and copy its four numbers (RPM, ITPM, OTPM, monthly cap) plus today's date into your build log.
  2. Break it on purpose. On any free door from Chapter 03, fire trivial prompts rapidly until HTTP 429 appears. Record the wording and retry-after, then classify which dial tripped: burst, throughput, or daily.
  3. Ship-sized task. Add one compass row for a triage tactic you actually use: name, graded stability/legality, receipt URL, re-verify date.
§7

Self-check

Why does the cached-read exemption change how you architect prompts?

Cached reads don't count toward Anthropic's input-token ceiling, so repeated stable context stops consuming your throughput budget — caching discipline widens the wall without paying for a higher tier.

Name two reasons the "$100–200/month" Codex figure deserves suspicion.

Vendor-reported with no distribution, workload definition, or methodology — and the reporting party profits from adoption looking affordable. Order-of-magnitude framing only; check your own /status.

A relay has been up for six months without incident. Why is it still categorically unsafe?

Uptime is not permission. Its mechanics violate written terms — credential sharing and resale — and enforcement is a business decision, not a technical one. Documented OAuth reuse rests on a grant; a relay rests on not having been caught.

§9

Beyond this build

When spending $20 beats hours of optimization. Your evening is the expensive token. If a month of a modest subscription costs less than the hours you'd burn routing around walls, buy the month and ship instead. Do that math hourly before building plumbing to save pocket change.

Org procurement reality. Past a second teammate, keys, budgets, and data boundaries need an owner — organizations have a department for this. "Which tier, whose card, what may leave" is a form, not a hack; knowing the grey-zone map lets you explain why the sanctioned door costs what it costs.

Ethics, one paragraph. Grey-zone tricks shift costs onto someone else: the vendor that must enforce, other users sharing the pool, the maintainer of a proxy that gets taken down, future-you inheriting a banned account. Nothing here is illegal to read; several things violate contracts you would be signing. Calibration is the skill this chapter teaches. The choice — and the account it rides on — stay yours.
